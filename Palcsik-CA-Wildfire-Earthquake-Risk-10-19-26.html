Every populated California ZIP code scored for wildfire and earthquake, with inland flooding and landslide as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. Earthquake is the hazard most California homes share, highest in the dense, high-value cores of Los Angeles and the Bay Area; wildfire is what sets the foothill, canyon and mountain ZIPs apart, from the Sierra and the North State to the edges of the Inland Empire and San Diego County, and it is the peril behind most of today's California home insurance availability questions. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for Home leads, section 05 covers Southern California, with the Bay Area and the Sacramento area alongside, and section 06 matches the tiers against the 3,196 GOAL Home leads delivered from January 1 to October 19, 2026. The full ZIP-by-ZIP list ships as a spreadsheet with this report.

03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • Why these twoFEMA's expected annual building loss for California ranks inland flooding ($11,558 million a year, 49%), earthquake ($10,194 million a year, 43%), wildfire ($1,706 million a year, 7%); everything else together is under 2%. Earthquake and wildfire are the two perils that set a California home quote apart, and wildfire is behind most of today's availability questions. Inland flooding ranks first in FEMA's model but is excluded from a standard home policy and quoted separately, so it stays in the score as a smaller input and in the spreadsheet.
  • EarthquakeNRI earthquake risk: modeled shaking likelihood times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. California sets the top of the national scale and half of its housing sits in ZIPs scoring 93.0 or more, so the cut points are set within California. Dense, high-value urban cores score highest.
  • WildfireNRI wildfire risk: modeled burn likelihood and intensity times the value exposed. The tier rules also use the share of a ZIP's residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for wildfire, which picks out the wildland-urban edge.
  • Smaller inputsNRI inland flooding and landslide risk: added to the composite score at lower weight; shown in the spreadsheet with heat wave.
  • Not scoredHail is not a meaningful California peril: half of California housing sits in ZIPs scoring 20.7 or less nationally for it, and 0.2% of residents live in tracts FEMA rates high for it, so it is left out.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (year built, foundation, seismic retrofit, roof, defensible space, distance to wildland) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeEarthquake score 98+, or a wildfire score of 95+ with 50%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for wildfire
  • HighEarthquake score 96+, 20%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 82+
  • ModerateEarthquake score 93+, 5%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 75+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme is sized to mark about one California home in nine (11.3% of housing). The earthquake arm (98+) covers 4.7% of housing; the wildfire arm covers 6.8%, mostly smaller foothill and mountain ZIPs where most residents live in high-wildfire tracts.

04

Home leads: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets an appetite check · High tier gets a construction and deductible review

Check appetite · Extreme · 630 ZIPs · 11.3% of CA housing Construction and deductible review · High · 456 ZIPs · 28.7% No change · 677 ZIPs · 60.0%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 11.3% of California's housing from Home; adding the High tier would bring it to 40.0%. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. Several California carriers have limited new home business in recent years, and appetite can differ by ZIP and by carrier, so we recommend excluding only the ZIPs where your carriers are not writing new home business today, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: year built and foundation, earthquake coverage and how its deductible works, and roof, vents and defensible space where wildfire applies.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm your carriers are writing new home business in the ZIP before the first call. In earthquake ZIPs, ask the year built and whether the house is bolted to its foundation, and walk through earthquake coverage and its deductible. In wildfire ZIPs, ask about roof material, ember-resistant vents, defensible space and road access. Where no carrier you represent is writing, the California FAIR Plan paired with a difference-in-conditions policy is the market of last resort.
  • HighConstruction and deductible review. Ask the year built and about any seismic retrofit, offer earthquake coverage and explain how its percentage deductible works; in wildfire-edge ZIPs add the roof, vent and defensible-space questions.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; a sentence on earthquake coverage is still worth it almost anywhere in California.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
Two kinds of Extreme ZIP

Southern California

Where most of your leads come from · with the Bay Area and the Sacramento area

Southern California
The six counties' 670 ZIPs: 142 Extreme, 192 High, 207 Moderate and 129 Low. Half of the area's housing sits in ZIPs scoring 93.2 or more for earthquake, and 23% of it in ZIPs where 20% or more of residents live in high-wildfire tracts. Of its 334 High and Extreme ZIPs, 96 are there on earthquake, 176 on wildfire, 36 on both earthquake and wildfire and 26 on the combined score. The largest earthquake-driven Extreme ZIPs are 92211 (Riverside), 90005 (Los Angeles), 90057 (Los Angeles) and 90017 (Los Angeles). The largest wildfire-driven High and Extreme ZIPs are 92618 (Orange), 93065 (Ventura), 92503 (Riverside) and 92592 (Riverside), at the wildland edge.
Bay Area and Sacramento
The nine Bay Area counties' 295 ZIPs: 79 Extreme, 119 High, 78 Moderate and 19 Low, with 55% of housing in High or Extreme ZIPs; of its 198 High and Extreme ZIPs, 103 are there on earthquake, 34 on wildfire, 29 on both earthquake and wildfire and 32 on the combined score. The Sacramento area (Sacramento, Placer, El Dorado and Yolo) is lower on earthquake: its 124 ZIPs are 44 Extreme, 19 High, 8 Moderate and 53 Low, with 16% of housing in High or Extreme ZIPs; of its 63 High and Extreme ZIPs, 43 are there on wildfire, 6 on both earthquake and wildfire and 14 on the combined score, mostly in the Placer and El Dorado foothills.

07

Earthquake and wildfire in the conversation

Questions that make a California home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Earthquake68% of California housing sits in ZIPs scoring 90+ for earthquake nationally. A standard home policy does not cover earthquake damage; it is added as separate coverage, from the California Earthquake Authority or a private market, with its own percentage deductible. Offering it on the first call, and explaining the deductible plainly, builds trust and avoids surprises.
  • ConstructionYear built, foundation type and any seismic retrofit (bolting, cripple-wall bracing) are the questions that shape an earthquake quote, especially for older homes built on raised foundations.
  • WildfireIn wildfire ZIPs, ask about roof material, ember-resistant vents, defensible space and road access on the first call. Carriers increasingly ask about home-hardening and mitigation steps, so having the answers ready keeps the quote moving and shows the client you know their area.
  • AvailabilityWhere a client has been non-renewed or cannot find coverage, be ready to explain the options plainly: confirm which of your carriers are writing new home business in the ZIP, and, where none are, the California FAIR Plan with a difference-in-conditions policy.
  • Flooding48.9% of Californians live in tracts FEMA rates high for inland flooding. Flood is excluded from a standard home policy, so a flood quote is a natural follow-up near rivers and in burn-scar areas.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (earthquake, wildfire, inland flooding, landslide, heat wave, hail), all 9,106 California tracts. The December 2025 release reports river flooding as inland flooding. Peril ranking from the same release's expected annual building loss, summed across California tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside California are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024.
  • LeadsAll 2026 GOAL prospects for this account, Jan 1 to Oct 19, 2026 (3,196 Home, plus 66 Commercial and Auto leads that these pages leave out), counted by ZIP. 3,179 of 3,196 Home leads matched a populated California ZIP.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Year built, foundation, roof, vents and defensible space decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first. Carrier appetite changes often and is not part of this data.
  • FilesPalcsik-CA-ZIP-Data-10-19-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
